How to calculate your real profit on Swiggy and Zomato

Most cloud kitchen owners know their food cost. Far fewer know what a single order actually leaves in the bank once the platform has taken its share. Here is the simple way to work it out, dish by dish.

The five things that come out of every order

  1. Discount you fund. If you run "40% off up to ₹80", that money comes out of your side, usually before commission is worked out.
  2. Commission. A percentage of the food value after discount. It varies by city, contract and whether you've opted into extra visibility, so use the number in your own agreement.
  3. Payment or collection fee. A small percentage on what the customer pays.
  4. Fixed per-order fees. Anything charged per order regardless of value, for example long distance or platform fee shares.
  5. GST on those fees. Platforms charge 18% GST on their services to you. If you aren't claiming it back as input credit, it's a real cost.

GST on the food itself (5% for most restaurants) is collected from the customer and paid to the government by the platform. It isn't your income, so leave it out of profit entirely.

A worked example: a ₹199 plate of momos

Say your food cost is ₹52, packaging is ₹14, commission is 25%, payment fee is 2%, and you're running no discount.

Menu price₹199.00
Commission (25% of ₹199)−₹49.75
Payment fee (2% of ₹208.95 the customer pays)−₹4.18
GST on fees (18% of ₹53.93)−₹9.71
Payout to you₹135.36
Food + packaging−₹66.00
Profit you keep₹69.36 (34.9%)

Now switch on a "40% off up to ₹80" offer. The discount is ₹79.60, commission drops to ₹29.85 on the ₹119.40 left, and after fees and GST your payout is about ₹81. Take out ₹66 of costs and you keep roughly ₹15 an order. Same dish, same kitchen, a fifth of the profit.

Finding the price that hits your margin

Because every fee scales with price, you can't just add your target margin on top of food cost. The cleaner way is to ask: at what menu price does profit equal, say, 20% of that price? PlateMath solves this for every dish and channel, including discount caps, and rounds up to the nearest rupee.

A quick checklist before you change prices

  • Pull one week of payout reports and check the effective commission you actually paid, not the headline rate.
  • Include packaging, sauces and cutlery. On low ticket items they can be a quarter of your cost.
  • Model your discount campaigns per channel. A dish that's fine at full price can lose money on every discounted order.
  • Compare with your direct channel. Even after delivery cost, the same dish often earns far more.
